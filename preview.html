Diversity in the Living World

ANCIENT WISDOM ON NATURE
Flat illustration · trees, shade and shared fruit
“
छायामन्यस्य कुर्वन्ति तिष्ठन्ति स्वयमातपे।
फलान्यपि परार्थाय वृक्षाः सत्पुरुषा इव॥
(सुभाषित)
Trees stand in the Sun and give shade to others. Their fruits are also for others. Likewise, good people bear all hardships and bring welfare to others. They give to others whatever they have earned.
(Subhashita — a wise saying)
”

📖 Historical Facts — Ancient Indian Science

Selfless Nature — Trees and Living Diversity

This Subhashita compares trees to good people because both give without expecting anything back. Trees take in sunlight, yet share their shade; they grow fruit, yet the fruit feeds birds, animals, and people. This ancient saying reflects an early understanding that every living thing in a habitat depends on and supports others — the same idea we now study as biodiversity and ecological balance.

ANCIENT SOURCE

Subhashita

A collection of short Sanskrit verses containing wise sayings, passed down through generations to teach moral and natural lessons.

SCIENTIFIC CONNECTION

Ecosystem services

Trees provide shade, oxygen, and fruit that support other organisms — an early recognition of how one living thing sustains many others.

NATURE CONNECTION

Interdependence

No tree grows for itself alone. Birds nest in its branches, insects feed on its leaves, and animals eat its fruit — a living web of diversity.

LIFE LESSON

Give without expecting return

Just as trees endure heat to offer shade, the verse teaches that true goodness lies in helping others without seeking reward.

MODERN RELEVANCE

Conserving biodiversity

Protecting trees protects entire communities of organisms that rely on them — a reminder of why forests matter to every species, including us.

DID YOU KNOW?

One tree, many homes

A single large tree can shelter hundreds of species — insects, birds, fungi, and small mammals — showing diversity within a single living organism.
