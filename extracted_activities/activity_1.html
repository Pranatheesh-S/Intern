Look across your table: what do these animals share, and where do they differ?
You'll find great variety — some animals make their home (habitat) on the ground, others up in trees; fish stay in water, while a frog is at home both on land and in water. Their foods and their movements differ just as widely.
Back home, sketch what you saw, or build a scrapbook with fallen leaves, flowers and stray feathers, and write down everything you noticed.
Even on the way to and from school, keep your eyes open — add any new plant or animal to your tables.

📝Quick practice

Q1. Which animal is at home both on land and in water?
A frog can live on land and in water — its legs and moist skin suit both.
Q2. A crow eats insects, scraps and grain. This makes it…
An animal that eats both plants and animals is an omnivore — like the crow.
Q3. What is the right way to study animals on a nature walk?
A good observer watches and records without disturbing any animal or its home.
